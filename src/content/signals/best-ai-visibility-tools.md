The short answer: for most established businesses in 2026, [Peec AI](https://peec.ai)
is the right starting tracker, [Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com) is the
enterprise pick, [Otterly.ai](https://otterly.ai) is the budget entry, and if
you already pay for Semrush, its AI Toolkit is the add-on to switch on. But
the tool question is the second question. The first is whether you have
anything to track: a tracker measures your share of answer, it does not move
it.

We say that as practitioners. Our [GEO service](/ai-seo/) runs answer audits
across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, and Google AI Overviews
every week, and tooling roundups from
[Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-visibility-tool/) and
[Frase](https://www.frase.io/blog/the-10-best-ai-visibility-tools-in-2026)
now list a dozen-plus contenders. Here's how we'd actually choose.

## The field, matched to your stage

| Tool | Best for | The honest take |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peec AI | Growing brands and agencies | Clean share-of-answer and competitor tracking without enterprise overhead. The default recommendation. |
| Profound | Enterprise | The big-picture platform: answer volumes, citation sources, agent analytics. Priced accordingly. |
| Otterly.ai | Budget-conscious starters | Solid prompt monitoring; lighter on the "what to do about it" layer. |
| Semrush AI Toolkit | Teams already on Semrush | One toggle instead of one more vendor; visibility data next to your SEO data. |
| SE Ranking AI tools | Small teams on a suite | AI visibility bolted onto an affordable rank tracker. |
| A manual audit | Everyone, first | Ask the six engines your buyers' questions and read the answers. Free, and more revealing than any dashboard. |

Two things the roundups agree on, and our audit work confirms. First,
[coverage differs](https://www.frase.io/blog/the-10-best-ai-visibility-tools-in-2026):
every serious tool tracks ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini; support for
Claude, Copilot, and AI Overviews varies, so check the engine list against
where your buyers actually ask. Second,
["share of answer" is not standardized](https://www.therankmasters.com/insights/ai-visibility/best-ai-visibility-tools-share-of-voice):
some tools count any mention, others only recommendations, and only some
separate a positive citation from a dismissive one. When you compare tools
on a trial, run the same prompt set through both and read the raw answers
before you trust either number.

## What none of them can do

A tracker is a scoreboard. The score moves when the inputs change, and the
inputs live outside the dashboard:

1. **The sources engines trust.** AI answers lean on review sites,
   directories, and press. If those don't know you exist, no tool fixes it.
2. **Your entity data.** Inconsistent names, descriptions, and profiles
   across the web teach engines uncertainty, and engines don't cite what
   they're unsure about.
3. **Liftable content.** Engines quote pages that answer questions
   directly, in their first paragraph, with numbers. Most business sites
   still bury the answer.

That's the actual work of generative engine optimization, and it's why we
lead engagements with an [answer audit](/ai-seo/) instead of a dashboard
login. Measure, fix, then track: in that order, the tools above are
genuinely useful. In any other order, they're a subscription to watching
yourself lose.
